# Your Role as an Entry-Level UX Designer in a Design Sprint

As an entry-level UX designer, I may not lead the entire Design Sprint, but I can contribute to every phase of the sprint.

## 1. Understand

- Learn about the users and their problems.
- Listen to experts and team members.
- Take notes and ask questions to better understand the design challenge.

## 2. Ideate

- Participate in brainstorming activities.
- Share my own ideas and possible solutions.
- Work with the team to explore different approaches.

## 3. Decide

- Participate in discussions about the ideas.
- Vote for the most promising solutions.
- Help the team decide which ideas should move forward.
- Help plan how the chosen idea will be tested.

## 4. Prototype

- Help create the prototype.
- Suggest ideas and improvements.
- Review the prototype with the team.
- Help make sure the prototype is ready for user testing.

## 5. Test

- Observe users interacting with the prototype.
- Interview users and collect their feedback.
- Identify usability problems.
- Use the feedback to help the team improve the design.

## Key Takeaway

As an entry-level UX designer, I don't have to run the entire Design Sprint. Instead, I actively contribute throughout the process:

**Understand → Ideate → Decide → Prototype → Test**

My role is to **learn, contribute ideas, collaborate with the team, help create solutions, and learn from users.**
