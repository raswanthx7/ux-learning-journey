## 1. What is Empathy in UX?

**Empathy** is the ability to understand another person's feelings, thoughts, needs, and perspective.

In UX design, empathy means trying to understand the user's experience instead of making assumptions about what the user needs.

### Key idea

> **Don't design based on what you think users need. Understand what users actually need first.**

Empathy helps designers:
- Understand user needs
- Discover pain points
- Understand user behavior
- Make better design decisions
- Avoid unnecessary revisions later

---

## 2. How to Empathize with Users

### 2.1 Ask Lots of Questions

Don't assume what users want.

Use questions beginning with:
- **What...**
- **How...**
- **Why...**

The purpose is to understand the user's perspective more deeply.

---

### 2.2 Become More Observant

Pay attention to more than the user's words.

Observe:
- What users do
- How they interact with a product
- Where they hesitate
- Where they make mistakes
- Their physical or behavioral cues

**Important:**

> What users say and what users actually do may not always be the same.

---

### 2.3 Be an Active Listener

Active listening means:
- Fully concentrating on what the user says
- Understanding what they mean
- Remembering important details
- Avoiding distractions
- Not planning your next response while they are talking

The goal is to collect honest and useful information rather than trying to prove your own idea.

---

### 2.4 Request Input

Get feedback from a **variety of relevant users**, rather than relying only on friends or colleagues.

Friends may give overly positive or biased feedback.

Prefer **open-ended questions**, such as:

> "What was difficult about this experience?"

instead of:

> "Do you like this?"

---

### 2.5 Have an Open Mind

Everyone has biases.

A **bias** is favoring or having prejudice toward something or someone based on limited information.

As a UX designer:
- Recognize your assumptions
- Set them aside during research
- Don't try to prove your idea is correct
- Let users' experiences guide your understanding

### Key mindset

> **Research to discover, not to confirm your assumptions.**

---

### 2.6 Keep Current on UX Research

UX research changes as our understanding of human behavior and psychology develops.

Designers should:
- Follow UX researchers
- Join UX communities
- Keep learning about research methods
- Stay aware of new findings

# 3. Empathy vs. Sympathy

### Empathy

Understanding another person's feelings or thoughts from their perspective.

### Sympathy

Showing concern or compassion for someone without necessarily experiencing or understanding their emotions in the same way.

### UX takeaway

A UX designer needs to **understand the user's experience**, not simply feel sorry for the user.

---

# 4. User Interviews

One way to understand users directly is through **user interviews**.

The purpose of an interview is to learn about:
- User needs
- Behaviors
- Experiences
- Problems
- Pain points
- Current ways of completing tasks

### Important

An interview should not start with:

> "What feature do you want?"

Instead, try to understand:

> **"What are you currently doing, and what is difficult about it?"**

---

# 5. Recruiting Research Participants

Before recruiting participants, define **why you are conducting the interviews**.

## 5.1 Determine Interview Goals

Ask yourself:

### What do I want to learn?

What information do I need from users?

### What user problems or pain points do I need to understand?

What difficulties might users experience?

### What characteristics should participants have?

Who actually experiences the problem you are researching?

### Why those characteristics?

Why are these people relevant to the research?

### How much information do I need?

Do I have enough participants and perspectives to identify useful patterns and avoid relying on one person's experience?

---

## 5.2 Screener Survey

A **screener survey** is a list of questions used to determine whether potential participants meet the requirements of a research study.

Example:

If the research requires people who regularly send invoices, a screener could ask about:
- Whether they own/run a small business
- Whether they provide services
- Whether they send invoices
- How frequently they send invoices

### Simple definition

> **Screener = a filter for finding suitable research participants.**

A screener is useful in real-world UX research, although it may not be required for the course project.

---

# 6. Representative Sample

A **representative sample** is a smaller group of participants that aims to reflect important characteristics of the larger target population.

When recruiting, avoid relying on only one narrow type of user.

Consider relevant differences such as:
- Age
- Gender
- Ability
- Experience
- Background
- Different ways of using the product

### Goal

> Get a diverse enough group to understand different user experiences and reduce research bias.

---

# 7. Finding Interview Participants

Possible sources include:

### People you already know
- Friends
- Family
- Classmates
- Colleagues
- Professional contacts

### People outside your network
- LinkedIn
- Social media
- Online communities
- Interest-based groups
- Reddit and other relevant communities

### Real-world professional options
- Third-party recruiting agencies
- Paid research platforms

Recruiting agencies can save time and help reach diverse users, but they cost money.

---

# 8. Contacting Participants

A recruitment email/message should generally include:

1. Greeting and introduction
2. Brief description of the project
3. Reason for contacting them
4. Interview location
5. Date and time
6. Expected interview setup
7. Recording/consent information
8. How to confirm participation
9. Incentive, if available
10. Closing and name

### After confirmation

Send reminders:
- About a week before the interview
- The night before the interview

This helps reduce no-shows.

# 9. Applying This to the Invoice App Project

### Project

> **Design an app and a responsive website that help small business owners send and track invoices for their services.**

For this project, don't immediately assume what the users' biggest problem is.

Instead, investigate their current experience.

### Think about three areas:

**Tools**
- What tools do they currently use?

**Steps**
- What is their current process for creating, sending, and tracking an invoice?

**Problems**
- Where does that process become difficult, confusing, slow, or frustrating?

### Research mindset

Start with:

> **"How do small business owners currently handle invoicing?"**

Then investigate the details.

Don't jump straight to:

> "I'll build feature X because I think they need it."

---

# 10. Thinking About Interview Goals

A useful way to think about your research goal is:

**What do I want to understand?**
↓
**What behavior/process am I investigating?**
↓
**What problems might exist?**
↓
**Who experiences this problem?**
↓
**What evidence do I need from interviews?**

For the invoice project, possible areas to investigate are:

**Creating invoices → Sending invoices → Tracking invoices → Following up → Receiving payment**

You can explore these connected areas while still keeping the research focused on the invoicing experience.

---

# ⭐ Core Takeaways

### Empathy
Understand users instead of assuming what they need.

### Interview
Talk directly to users to learn about their experiences.

### Goal
Decide what you need to learn before conducting interviews.

### Screener
Filter potential participants to find people who fit the research requirements.

### Representative sample
Include relevant diversity so your findings aren't based on one narrow group.

### Interview questions
Focus on users' **real experiences, behaviors, and problems**, rather than asking them to design your product for you.

### Most important mindset

> **Understand first → Define the problem → Then design the solution.**
