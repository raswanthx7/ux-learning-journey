---

## 1. Why Preparation Matters

Preparation helps UX designers make the most of the limited time they have with interview participants.

Whether the interview is conducted **in person or online**, preparing beforehand helps you:

- Keep the interview organized
- Use the available time effectively
- Avoid technical problems
- Ask useful questions
- Learn more about the participant's perspective
- Make the interview run smoothly

> **Key idea:** A successful user interview starts with good preparation.

---

## 2. Script the Interview Questions

Before conducting interviews, prepare a list of questions that will guide the conversation.

It is considered a best practice to keep the **core interview questions consistent for every participant**.

### Why keep questions consistent?

Using consistent questions makes it easier to:

- Compare answers between participants
- Identify common patterns
- Identify differences between users
- Avoid accidentally asking leading or unrelated questions

### Example

Instead of asking every participant completely different questions:

- User 1: "How do you create invoices?"
- User 2: "Do you find invoicing difficult?"
- User 3: "Would an invoice app be useful?"

Prepare a common set of questions such as:

1. How do you currently create invoices?
2. What tools do you use?
3. Can you walk me through the last time you created an invoice?
4. What parts of the process are difficult?
5. How do you track whether customers have paid?

The interview questions act as a **guide**, not a script that must be followed word-for-word.

---

## 3. Prepare Interview Supplies

Collect everything you may need before the interview.

Possible supplies include:

- Printed interview questions
- Computer or laptop
- Paper
- Pencil or pen
- Recording equipment
- Headphones
- Microphone
- Video-conferencing software

The exact supplies depend on whether the interview is conducted **in person or online**.

---

## 4. Test Your Technology

If you're using new equipment or technology, make sure you know how it works **before the interview begins**.

For an online interview, check:

- Video-conferencing platform
- Microphone
- Camera
- Internet connection
- Recording setup
- Screen sharing
- Participant access

### Important

Don't wait until the interview starts to discover that something doesn't work.

> **Test your technology before the actual interview.**

---

## 5. Have a Backup Plan

Technology can fail, so a good UX designer prepares for problems.

Possible problems:

- Internet connection fails
- Microphone stops working
- Video doesn't work
- Participant can't access the meeting
- Recording fails
- Interview platform has problems

### Example Backup Plan

**Primary:** Online video interview  
↓  
**Problem:** Video platform doesn't work  
↓  
**Backup:** Phone call

Having the participant's phone number or another way to contact them can be useful.

> **Key idea:** Always have a simple Plan B.

---

## 6. Research the Users

Before the interview, review the information you already have about the participant.

This information might include:

- Name
- Demographic information
- Relevant experience with the product
- Previous experience related to the problem
- How they found the interview
- Answers from a screener survey

### Why research participants beforehand?

It helps you:

- Understand who you're talking to
- Avoid asking questions you already know the answers to
- Prepare relevant follow-up questions
- Better understand their answers

---

## 7. Review Screener Survey Answers

If you used a **screener survey** to select participants, review their answers before the interview.

### Example

Suppose a participant said:

> "I've been running my business for five years and currently use Excel to create invoices."

Instead of asking:

> "Do you use Excel?"

You can ask:

> "Can you walk me through the last time you used Excel to create an invoice?"

This allows you to use your interview time more effectively.

---

## 8. Practice Before the Interview

Practice delivering your questions before conducting the real interview.

Practicing helps you:

- Become more comfortable speaking
- Find awkward wording
- Identify confusing questions
- Improve the flow of the interview
- Reduce nervousness
- Make the interview run smoothly

### Example

Instead of:

> "What challenges do you experience when utilizing your current invoicing workflow?"

Use simpler language:

> "What's frustrating about the way you create invoices today?"

The goal is to make questions **clear, natural, and easy for the participant to understand**.

---

# UX Interview Preparation Checklist

Before starting the interview, check:

- [ ] I know what I want to learn
- [ ] I prepared my interview questions
- [ ] My core questions are consistent across participants
- [ ] I have all necessary supplies
- [ ] I tested my equipment
- [ ] I tested the online interview platform if applicable
- [ ] I confirmed the participant can access the technology
- [ ] I have a backup plan
- [ ] I reviewed the participant's information
- [ ] I reviewed their screener survey responses
- [ ] I practiced asking the questions

---

# Important UX Principle

Preparation does **not** mean preparing for users to confirm your assumptions.

The goal of an interview is to **learn from users**, not prove that your idea is correct.

### Bad approach

> "Would automatic invoice reminders be useful to you?"

This assumes that automatic reminders are the solution.

### Better approach

> "How do you currently follow up with customers who haven't paid?"

This allows the user to explain their actual behavior and problems.

---

# Key Takeaway

Preparing for a UX interview involves four major areas:

### 1. Script
Prepare consistent interview questions.

### 2. Supplies
Prepare your equipment and materials.

### 3. Research
Understand the participant before the interview.

### 4. Practice
Practice delivering the questions so the interview runs smoothly.

> **Prepare well → Ask clearly → Listen carefully → Learn from the user**
