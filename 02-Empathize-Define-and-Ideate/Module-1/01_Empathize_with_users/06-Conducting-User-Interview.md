# The Four Steps of a User Interview

A UX user interview can be divided into four main steps:

1. **Meet the participant**
2. **Conduct the interview**
3. **Take notes**
4. **Wrap up the interview**

> **Simple mental model:** Meet → Interview → Notes → Wrap Up

---

# 1. Meet the Participant

The first step is to meet the participant and make them feel comfortable.

Meeting a stranger can sometimes feel awkward, so the UX designer's responsibility is to create a relaxed and professional environment.

## Build Rapport

Rapport means creating a **friendly and professional relationship** with the participant.

You can start with light conversation, such as:

> "How's your day going?"

You should also thank the participant for taking the time to participate.

### Why is rapport important?

It helps participants:

- Feel comfortable
- Feel respected
- Feel that their time is valued
- Become more willing to share their honest experiences

---

## Review Legal and Consent Details

Before the interview begins, review any legal information the participant needs to know.

This may include:

- Consent for audio or video recording
- Required consent forms
- Age verification
- Permission from a parent or guardian when applicable

### Important

Always make sure participants understand and agree to any required recording or research consent before proceeding.

---

## Gather Basic Participant Details

Collect relevant information about the participant.

This might include:

- Name
- Demographic information
- Relevant experience
- Experience with the product
- Other information relevant to the research

Starting with simple questions can help the participant become more comfortable.

---

## Tell Participants There Are No Right or Wrong Answers

Participants should understand that the interview is about **their experience and opinions**.

You want them to feel comfortable giving honest answers.

> **Key idea:** You're interested in what the participant really thinks and experiences, not a "correct" answer.

---

# 2. Conduct the Interview

Once the participant is comfortable, begin the actual interview.

There are three important things to remember:

- Follow interview etiquette
- Ask open-ended questions
- Ask follow-up questions

---

## Follow Interview Etiquette

When asking questions:

- Speak clearly
- Keep questions concise
- Remain professional
- Stay respectful
- Don't judge the participant's answers

Even if the participant gives an unexpected or negative answer, remain neutral.

### Example

Participant:

> "I hate using this app."

Instead of:

> "Really? But most people like it."

Ask:

> "Can you tell me more about what makes it frustrating?"

The goal is to understand the user's perspective rather than defend the product.

---

# Ask Open-Ended Questions

Open-ended questions allow participants to explain their thoughts and experiences in more detail.

## Closed Question

> "Do you like online shopping?"

Possible answers:

> "Yes."

or

> "No."

This gives you limited information.

## Open-Ended Question

> "What do you like and dislike about online shopping?"

The participant can now explain:

- What they like
- What they dislike
- Why they feel that way
- Problems they experience

---

## Examples of Better Interview Questions

### Closed

> "Do you find invoicing difficult?"

### Open-ended

> "What is difficult about your invoicing process?"

---

### Closed

> "Do you use Excel?"

### Open-ended

> "How do you currently manage your invoices?"

---

### Leading

> "Would automatic payment reminders be useful?"

### Better

> "How do you currently follow up with customers who haven't paid?"

> **Key principle:** Ask about the user's existing behavior and experience before jumping to your proposed solution.

---

# Ask Follow-Up Questions

Don't treat the interview like a questionnaire where you simply move from Question 1 to Question 2.

Pay attention to interesting things the participant says and explore them further.

### Example

Participant:

> "I spend a lot of time checking whether customers have paid."

Instead of immediately moving to the next question, ask:

> "Can you walk me through how you check?"

Then:

> "How often do you do that?"

Then:

> "What's the most frustrating part?"

Then:

> "Can you tell me about the last time that happened?"

This helps uncover deeper pain points.

### Simple Pattern

**Interesting answer**

↓

**Follow-up question**

↓

**More detail**

↓

**Underlying problem**

---

# 3. Take Notes

Taking notes is the third step of conducting a user interview.

It can be difficult to remember everything from a long conversation.

Important details can easily be forgotten after the interview.

Therefore, take notes during the interview.

---

# Capture Compelling Quotes

Pay attention to statements that strongly communicate how the participant thinks or feels.

### Example

> "I have to check three different places before I know whether someone has paid."

This could be a valuable quote because it clearly communicates a potential pain point.

Compelling quotes can later be used in:

- Empathy maps
- User testimonies
- Research findings
- Design presentations

---

# Document Observations

What users **do** can be just as important as what they **say**.

Pay attention to:

- Mood
- Facial expressions
- Body language
- Behavior
- Hesitation
- Confusion
- Frustration
- Excitement

### Example

A participant says:

> "The process is fine."

But while demonstrating the process, they repeatedly hesitate and appear frustrated.

That observation could be important.

> **Key idea:** Pay attention to both the participant's words and their behavior.

---

# Record the Interview

Recording an interview can be useful, especially when you're learning how to conduct UX research.

A recording allows you to:

- Revisit the interview later
- Check exact statements
- Find details you missed
- Take additional notes
- Review important moments

### Important

Always ask for the participant's permission before recording.

> **Never assume that you can record the participant.**

---

# 4. Wrap Up the Interview

The final step is to properly close the interview.

Don't abruptly end the conversation.

Give the participant an opportunity to share anything else they think is important.

---

## Ask for Final Thoughts

You can ask:

> "Is there anything else you'd like to add?"

or:

> "Is there anything we didn't discuss that you think is important?"

Participants may remember new information or share thoughts they didn't mention earlier.

This can lead to additional insights.

---

## Thank the Participant

Always thank the participant for:

- Their time
- Their participation
- Their opinions
- Their contribution to the research

Make sure they understand that their input is valuable to the design process.

---

## Provide Incentives

If you promised an incentive for participating in the interview, provide it during the wrap-up process.
