## What is a Design Sprint?

A Design Sprint is a time-boxed, collaborative process used to solve an important design challenge quickly.

The goal is to:
- Understand the challenge
- Generate possible solutions
- Decide on a promising solution
- Build a realistic prototype
- Test it with users

A traditional Design Sprint is often completed in 5 days, although sprints can also be shorter.

---

## 5 Phases of a Design Sprint

### 1. Understand 🔍

Understand the:
- Design challenge
- Users
- Business context
- Existing research
- Current user experience

**Goal:** Clearly understand what we are trying to solve.

---

### 2. Ideate 💡

Generate many possible solutions.

Methods can include:
- How Might We (HMW)
- Crazy 8s
- Solution Sketches
- User Journey Mapping
- Lightning Talks

**Goal:** Create many possible solutions without immediately choosing one.

---

### 3. Decide 🎯

Review the ideas and decide which solution is most promising.

Consider:
- User value
- Business requirements
- Feasibility
- Potential impact

**Goal:** Choose the solution to prototype.

---

### 4. Prototype 🖥️

Build a realistic prototype of the selected solution.

The prototype does NOT need to be the final product.

It only needs to be realistic enough for users to experience and test.

**Goal:** Create something that can be tested with users.

---

### 5. Test 👥

Put the prototype in front of real users.

Observe:
- What users do
- Where they struggle
- What they understand
- What they like/dislike

Then use the feedback to improve the solution.

**Goal:** Learn whether the solution actually works for users.

# When to Use a Design Sprint

A Design Sprint can be useful:

### 1. At the start of a new project
When the team needs to define the product direction or create a shared vision.

### 2. When time is critical
When the team needs to make an important decision quickly.

### 3. At an impasse, roadblock, or fork
When the team is stuck or has multiple possible directions.

### 4. After new research or insights
When new information creates an opportunity to explore and test a solution quickly.

---

# When NOT to Use a Design Sprint

Don't use a Design Sprint when:

- You don't have enough user research or understanding of your customers.
- The product direction is already clear and you simply need dedicated design time.
- Leadership does not support the sprint.

---

# Sprint Challenge

Every Design Sprint focuses on **one clearly defined Sprint Challenge**.

The challenge acts as the team's **north star** and keeps everyone focused.

Examples:

- Redesign a user flow to increase conversion.
- Explore new ways for users to discover products.
- Improve the process for approving new projects.
- Define the vision for a new product.

---

# Sprint Team

Design Sprints usually involve a cross-functional team.

Possible participants:

- UX Designer
- UX Researcher
- Product Manager
- Developer
- Business/Leadership
- Other important stakeholders

A typical team size is around **5–7 people**.

# Design Sprint vs Design Thinking

| Design Thinking | Design Sprint |
|---|---|
| Broader UX problem-solving framework | Focused, time-boxed process |
| Empathize → Define → Ideate → Prototype → Test | Understand → Ideate → Decide → Prototype → Test |
| Can take longer depending on the project | Usually takes a few focused days |
| Helps deeply understand users and define problems | Helps rapidly explore and validate solutions |
| General framework | Specific method for an important design challenge |

# Simple Example: Happn

## Problem

Happn noticed that users were liking profiles but were not starting enough conversations.

## Design Sprint

**Understand:**  
Study the current user journey and learn from experts.

**Ideate:**  
Generate many ideas to increase interaction.

**Decide:**  
Choose the most promising solutions.

**Prototype:**  
Create high-fidelity prototypes.

**Test:**  
Test the prototypes with users.

One prototype was not understood by users, while another prototype was well received.

## Outcome

Happn built and released the validated concept and measured increased user engagement.

↓  

**Ideate → What could we do?**

↓  

**Decide → Which idea should we try?**

↓  

**Prototype → Can we make a realistic version?**

↓  

**Test → Does it work for users?**

## Key Takeaway

A Design Sprint is a **fast way to explore, prototype, and test a solution before investing heavily in building it.**
