## What is a Sprint Retrospective?

A **sprint retrospective** is a collaborative meeting held after a Design Sprint to review how the sprint went.

The goal is to **learn from the experience and improve future sprints**.

## When Does It Happen?

- Usually happens **immediately after the sprint**.
- This allows everyone to share their thoughts while the experience is still fresh.

## Two Key Questions

The retrospective focuses on two main questions:

1. **What went well?**
2. **What can be improved?**

## How Is It Conducted?

- The **Sprint Leader** guides the conversation.
- Everyone who participated in the sprint gets a chance to give feedback.
- Someone takes notes during the meeting.
- The team uses the feedback to make future sprints more productive.

## Why Is a Retrospective Important?

Retrospectives help teams:

- Improve teamwork
- Improve communication with clients
- Identify areas for individual growth
- Learn from mistakes
- Avoid repeating the same mistakes
- Make future sprints more productive

## Important Mindset

A retrospective is about **empowering, not shaming**.

If something didn't go well, the goal is not to blame someone. Instead, the team should identify what resources, tools, or changes could help everyone do better next time.

## Example

Suppose the team struggled to complete user testing on time.

During the retrospective:

- **What went well:** The team created the prototype successfully.
- **What can be improved:** User testing preparation started too late.
- **Next time:** Prepare testing questions and schedule testers earlier.

## Easy Way to Remember

**Design Sprint → Solve the problem**

**Sprint Retrospective → Improve the process**

> **Retrospective = Look back → Learn → Improve**
